# RQ3: controlled variation within a termination setting

This is a new, prospective single-host Kind experiment, separate from the historical Fast-X/Slow-X runs and their offline decision replay. The goal is to test whether a verified handler-entry event adds actionable information after conditioning the baseline on the known termination setting. The perturbation is deliberately injected, not an estimate of natural jitter or its prevalence.

## Frozen design before calibration

- Start from the hash-pinned November 17 physical runner and November 18 shared decision/recheck contract. Keep the pinned node and application images, one nonpreempting isolated node, the X/Y/Z resource requests, 24 MiB spare, Y at intent+3.55 s, Z at intent+3.80 s, the 8.5 s accounting window, and the 30 s termination grace period. Do not mutate other workloads.
- Known settings: X cleanup 2.9 s or 3.1 s, the same preStop command and Pod configuration within each setting. Before retirement, the fixture writes one of two timing tokens (0.00 or 1.55 s) into a fixture-only volume. The identical preStop command reads and sleeps for this workload disturbance before kubelet delivers SIGTERM. None of the three controllers receives the token, assigned stratum, or future-return ledger. The setting-aware arms may read the configured cleanup and setting key; the pooled arm uses one median over both settings.
- Calibration: 3 shuffled blocks x 2 settings x 2 disturbances = 12 new trials. Force the first additional retirement to instrument both release events; no calibration recheck. Use only these trials to estimate pooled and setting-specific median X release-from-intent, and setting-specific median of release minus handler entry minus cleanup. Use the midpoint of the scheduler-log bracket, not a physical RSS measurement. All 12 must complete with UID-verified Bind and verified signal available at the decision. In each setting/disturbance cell at least 2/3 must have a bounded X release strictly within (0, 1] s and before Z for zero disturbance, or strictly after 1 s and after Z for 1.55 s disturbance. Calibration gate failure blocks evaluation without changing the contract or retrying in place.
- Evaluation: separate fresh cluster; 4 randomized blocks x 2 settings x 2 disturbances x 3 physically executed policies = 48 trials. Pair arms by block and laboratory condition, not by Pod UID. Pooled Fixed uses the pooled median, Setting-specific Fixed the setting median, Signal the known setting's handler-based residual if the same UID was validated at or before decision, otherwise the setting median. At first decision and one Y+1 s recheck, each arm uses its own forecast; shared Pending check, eligibility/idle protection, actual reservation, Y submission and timeout handling. Forecasts at or before decision are excluded, as in the prior fixed/signal rule. The scheduler, not the forecast, frees capacity or binds Pods.
- Retrospective oracle: on the observed state, a verified X return interval on one side of the 1 s horizon defines a reference first action; do not infer unexecuted oracle latency, oracle reward, or an optimal policy. Also report ambiguity and missing intervals. A wait decision is not a timed reservation release. For setting-specific versus Signal, report initial and actual additional retirement, Y arrival-to-Bind and Y/Z HTTP completion durations, Z recreation, completion, and bounds on integrated reservation GB.s. Show each block/setting/disturbance, all effects and all assigned trials. Outcome differences compare separately run policies, not a deterministic same-realization replay.
- No post-evaluation thresholds or exclusion tuning. Mark noncomplete, missing UID, missing/ambiguous brackets, missing signal, and request timeout distinctly. Latencies use jointly scored pairs only; report their denominator and preserve the planned denominator. No claim of population prevalence, production improvement, or superiority from single-host, small-block results. Physical experiment, retrospective oracle and historical decision replay stay separated.
